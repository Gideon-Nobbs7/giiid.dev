I want you to write a highly personalized cover letter for a job application.

Your goal is NOT to simply rewrite my resume or repeat the job description. I want the letter to sound like it was written by a real engineer who understands the company, understands the problem they are trying to solve, and has actually built things that demonstrate they can contribute.

### Important writing philosophy

In this cover letter, DO NOT turn my skills into a shopping list.

Do NOT write things like:

"I have experience with Python, Django, PostgreSQL, Docker, AWS, Kafka, Redis, and RabbitMQ."

That belongs on my resume.

Instead, use my experience as evidence that I can solve the problems the company cares about.

The reader should finish the letter thinking:

"This person understands what we are building, has dealt with similar problems before, and I can trust them to take ownership and ship."

### Humanization requirements

The letter must feel genuinely human.

Avoid:

* Generic corporate language.
* Excessive buzzwords.
* "I am thrilled to apply..."
* "I am excited to leverage my skills..."
* "I believe I am the perfect candidate..."
* "I am confident that my skills and experience make me an ideal fit..."
* Empty statements about being passionate, hardworking, innovative, or results-driven unless supported by an actual example.
* Repeating the company's mission statement.
* Restating the job description paragraph by paragraph.
* Excessive use of words such as "scalable", "robust", "cutting-edge", "dynamic", "innovative", or "seamless".
* Making the letter sound like it was generated by AI.

Use natural sentence structures and varied paragraph lengths. It should sound like an engineer talking thoughtfully to another person.

### The central principle

Do not spend most of the letter telling the company what skills I have.

Instead:

1. Identify what the company is actually building.
2. Identify the real engineering or product problems behind that work.
3. Find the strongest parts of my experience that demonstrate I have encountered similar problems.
4. Connect those experiences naturally.
5. Show how I approach engineering problems.
6. Explain why this particular company and role makes sense for the next step in my career.
The company should feel like the subject of the letter, while my experience should serve as evidence that I can contribute.
### Storytelling approach
Where appropriate, use a structure similar to:
"The company is building X, which creates challenges around Y. I have encountered a similar problem while working on Z. In that system, I had to... This taught me... That is why I believe I can contribute to..."
Do not force this structure mechanically. The final letter should flow naturally.
### Evidence over claims
Whenever possible, replace claims about my abilities with evidence.
Weak:
"I am a strong problem solver.
Better:
"While working on an academic platform, I had to understand a custom authorization system that handled permissions across APIs and WebSocket connections. Working through that complexity taught me to look at authorization as a system rather than simply checking whether a user has access."
Do this throughout the letter.
### Seniority and honesty
I am applying as a backend/software engineer and should sound confident but not arrogant.
Do not exaggerate my experience.
Do not claim that I "architected", "led", "owned", or "built the entire system" unless the information I provide explicitly supports that claim.
If I contributed to a system, say I contributed.
If I worked alongside senior engineers, make that clear where relevant.
The goal is credibility.
### Use the job description intelligently
Analyze the job description and identify:
* What the company is actually trying to accomplish.
* What problems this role is likely expected to solve.
* What responsibilities matter most.
* What evidence from my experience best demonstrates I can handle those responsibilities.
* What parts of my background are unusually relevant to this particular company.
Do not simply mirror the language of the job description.
If the company emphasizes a technology, mention it only when I have actually used it or when my experience provides a meaningful connection.
### Metrics
Use metrics selectively.
A cover letter should not become a collection of statistics.
If a metric genuinely strengthens a story, use it. Otherwise, leave it for my resume.
Prioritize meaningful evidence such as:
* scale,
* complexity,
* reliability,
* performance improvements,
* business impact,
* ownership,
* difficult technical decisions,
* production problems solved.
### My unconventional background
My Environmental Science background should be used when it is genuinely relevant.
Do not force it into every application.
When the company works in areas such as sustainability, climate, agriculture, environmental technology, supply chains, or other domains connected to real-world systems, use this background as a differentiator.
The story should be:
"I understand the domain because I have lived/studied/worked around it, and I now bring software engineering skills to that domain."
Not:
"I studied Environmental Science, therefore I am passionate about technology."
### Length
Keep the final cover letter between 350 and 500 words unless the job application specifies another limit.
Every paragraph must earn its place.
### Final quality test
Before giving me the final letter, silently evaluate it using these questions:
1. Does this sound like a real person wrote it?
2. Does it clearly show that I understand what this company is building?
3. Does it demonstrate that I have solved related problems before?
4. Does it show evidence rather than simply making claims?
5. Does it avoid turning my skills into a technology shopping list?
6. Does it avoid repeating my resume?
7. Does it avoid simply paraphrasing the job description?
8. Does it make me sound like someone who can be trusted to take ownership and ship?
9. Does it contain anything that sounds obviously AI-generated or overly corporate?
10. If I removed my name, would this still sound specific to this company?

Now write the cover letter.
